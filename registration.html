Why it matters

Starting a party is an idea — registering one is a legal process

Before a party can field candidates anywhere in India, it must register with the Election Commission of India under the Representation of the People Act, 1951. The process is well-defined but unforgiving of missing paperwork, and typically takes three to six months to clear.

That's the work of a dedicated political party registration consultant: drafting a constitution that meets ECI expectations, preparing affidavits and office-bearer declarations, publishing the mandatory public notice, and following up with the Commission until approval comes through.

Before you apply

Eligibility & documents at a glance

A quick reference for what's typically needed before a party registration application goes to the ECI.

Who can register a party

  • Indian citizens residing in India, under Section 29A of the RP Act, 1951
  • A group with a shared ideology and defined leadership structure
  • First-time political aspirants as well as established community leaders
  • NRIs may support a party once it is formed by Indian leadership

Documents typically required

  • Party constitution and presidential affidavit
  • List of office bearers and member details
  • PAN card and bank account details
  • Proof of public notice publication in newspapers

Case notes

Frequently asked questions

Common questions founders ask before hiring a political party registration consultant in India.

01Who can register a political party in India?
Any Indian citizen or group of individuals with a shared ideology and leadership structure can apply to register a political party under Section 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951.
02What documents are required for registration?
Key documents include the party's constitution, an affidavit by the president, list of office bearers, PAN card, bank details, and proof of public notice publication. Consultants typically help prepare and organise all of these.
03How long does it take to get registered?
It usually takes three to six months for the Election Commission of India to review, seek clarifications, and approve a political party's registration, depending on documentation and other factors.
04Is newspaper publication mandatory?
Yes. The Election Commission requires a public notice to be published in national or regional newspapers as part of the mandatory disclosure process before registration is finalised.
05Can consultants help with party symbol allotment?
Yes, consultants typically assist in applying for a free or reserved symbol based on eligibility, helping frame the request in line with Election Commission guidelines for newly registered parties.
